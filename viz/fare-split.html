Fare split

A passenger buys a trip, not a flight. Their fare belongs to a market (where the trip starts and ends), but the airline wants to know what each route earned. When a trip uses two flights, the fare has to be cut in two. Pick a route. On the left is every market whose passengers flew it in February 2027, sized by the fares they paid. On the right is where that money is booked.

Same passengers, same fares, same total. Only the rule for cutting a two-flight fare changes.

What to notice. A route's revenue is not something anyone paid. It is assembled: all of the local fares, plus a slice of every connecting fare that passed through. The slice depends on a rule somebody chose, and switching the rule moves money between routes without a single passenger changing. That is why "how much does this route make" has more than one defensible answer, and why the warehouse carries several revenue columns for the same leg. Which way a route moves depends on whether it is usually the longer or the shorter flight of the pair: by miles the longer flight takes most of the fare, in equal halves they take the same.

Where the rules come from. "By miles" is what the synthetic airline does (synthetic_airline/warehouse_tables.py, coupon_cents). "By √miles" is my reading of what the real leg-revenue pipeline does: it prorates by the square root of revenue passenger miles (pipelines/gold/gold.fact_leg_rasm.py:302). "Equal halves" is there as a contrast and I have not seen it in the code. Limits: the grey totals on the right are only the money from passengers who also flew the picked route, not those routes' full revenue. Fares are one direction of travel, award tickets count as zero, and amounts are rebuilt from monthly totals, so they can differ from the simulator's per-ticket rounding by a few dollars.

Data: odysee-data-warehouse/src/utilities/synthetic_airline run at full scale for 1–28 February 2027, aggregated by onboarding/tools/viz_data.py --journeys. No client data.
