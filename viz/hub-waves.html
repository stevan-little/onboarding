Hub waves

Each row is one aircraft of the synthetic Odysee Air, and its height is how far it is from its hub. Out at a spoke is a crest, back at the hub is a trough. When troughs line up, passengers can change aircraft. Two dials let you pull the waves out of step and see what that costs: one moves the whole fleet, one moves a single aircraft.

      How to read the small charts. Each one is the answer to "what if I turned this dial to every position?", with a dot where the dial is now. A dot sitting on a peak means the schedule is already as good as that dial can make it. A higher point elsewhere on the curve means that, by this count alone, moving there would add short connections. What counts as a connection here: an arrival and a departure at the same hub, on different aircraft, not back to where the passenger came from, with a wait between the minimum connecting time (35 minutes, 60 if either end is outside the US) and 240 minutes, and a route no more than 1.5 times the direct distance. Those are the simulator's own rules. "Short" means a wait of 90 minutes or less. Limits: every connection counts the same, whether it serves a big market or nobody. Shifts wrap around midnight and ignore crews, gates and night curfews. This counts possibilities and says nothing about revenue.
