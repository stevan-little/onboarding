Hub retimer

The hub clock with a dial on every flight. Click a dot to pick a flight, then slide it earlier or later and watch its threads re-form. A red thread is a near miss: a connection that fails by a few minutes. Fixing one means moving the second flight later, and that move can break other connections and push the same aircraft's next flights. The table underneath ranks every near miss by what fixing it would really net.

Each row fixes one near miss by moving its second flight later by exactly the shortfall. "Makes" and "breaks" count every connection that changes across both hubs, knock-on flights included.

What your changes did

      What to notice. Fixing a near miss is rarely free. The flight you delay is also the second half of other connections, which get longer or drop out of the window, and the first half of connections at its destination. If the aircraft was due to turn around quickly, its next flight slips too and the effect travels down the day. The ranked table makes that trade visible: some fixes net several new connections, many net nothing, some lose more than they make. This is a toy version of what the Connectivity product's opportunities list and the optimizers weigh.

      Rules and limits. Connections follow the simulator's rules: minimum connecting time 35 minutes (60 if any airport involved is outside the US), no return to the origin, route no more than 1.5 times the direct distance, and the wait limit on the slider above. Knock-on is my assumption: an aircraft needs 30 minutes on the ground between flights, or its scheduled ground time if that was shorter, and a delayed flight pushes later ones only as far as that requires. Nothing here knows about crews, gates, curfews or demand, and every connection counts as one whether it serves a big market or nobody.
